I changed nothing in the repo: all measurement scripts ran from the scratchpad and `git status` is clean.

**Setup**
- M3 Pro (6 performance + 6 efficiency cores), Node 24.11.1. The load average was 8–56 during my runs (a VM at ~97% CPU, plus another process writing to `lectern/data/`). So Supertonic absolute times are above CLAUDE.md's ~3 s per chunk; I ran each comparison interleaved to cancel the drift.

**Top 8 (ranked by impact per effort)**

1. **Run Supertonic one request at a time** (engine, S)
- All providers share one `createSemaphore(2)` (`server/routes/tts.js:13,53`).
- onnxruntime-node wraps a synchronous native `run()` in `setImmediate` (`node_modules/onnxruntime-node/dist/backend.js:113-117`). So two Supertonic requests take turns step by step (`helper.js:246-257`) and both finish late.
- Both clients send chunks k and k+1 together (`queue.js:22-30`, `offscreen.js:54-62`).
- Measured (`st-ab.mjs`, 3 runs): chunk 0 is ready at 3.6 s when it runs alone first, versus 7.4 s when sent alongside chunk 1. Chunk 1 is ready at 8.5 s versus 7.7 s, so total throughput holds.
- Fix: one semaphore per provider, with `concurrency: 1` on the supertonic entry (`providers/index.js:30-37`).
- Risk: low. The 120 s timeout starts inside the semaphore, so queue wait doesn't count against it.

2. **Trim Supertonic leading and trailing silence** (engine, S)
- Measured (`silence.mjs`, decoded with macOS `afconvert`): each chunk has 500–560 ms of silence at the start and 520–730 ms at the end. That is 1,230 ms of dead air at every boundary, while pauses between sentences inside a chunk are 300–470 ms.
- Edge needs nothing: its boundaries are 470 ms against a natural 420–440 ms pause.
- Word timing is also off: `durationToWords` (`words.js:33-50`) spreads words across the padding, so the first word starts at 0 ms while speech begins at 560 ms.
- Fix (`supertonic.js:91-97`): cut from the first to the last 10 ms window above −45 dBFS, keep 100 ms / 250 ms pads, and time words over the kept span.
- Gain: about 0.9 s less dead air per boundary at 1×, 0.40–0.46 s faster time to first audio, 3–11% fewer samples to encode, and the highlight lines up with speech.
- Risk: low. Chunks already in the cache stay untrimmed unless the cache key is versioned.

3. **Cut Supertonic denoising steps from 8 to 5** (engine, S, needs a quality check)
- `supertonic.js:10` uses 8, which is also upstream's default (`nodejs/example_onnx.js`, `totalStep: 8`).
- The vector estimator is 90% of inference time (`st-bench.mjs`: 3,376 of 3,729 ms).
- Measured (`st-ab.mjs`, 220 chars, 3 runs): 5,285 → 3,450 ms (−35%); 4 steps gives 3,103 ms.
- Under load, 8 steps synthesizes at 0.38–0.48 of real time, too slow for gapless playback at 3× (which needs under 0.33). 5 steps measured 0.20–0.25.
- Risk: audio quality, which upstream has not endorsed. Do a blind A/B listen first, and version the cache key.

4. **Abort stale synthesis requests** (web, extension, engine; M)
- (a)/(h): after a seek, `pump()` does request the target first, but it counts stale fetches in `inFlight` (`queue.js:22-30`). So the target waits for an old request to finish.
- No request carries an AbortSignal (`api.js:30-31`, `offscreen.js:69-73`).
- Voice changes make it worse: `dispose()` (`queue.js:73-79`, called from `setVoice` in `player.js:274-286`) and `stop()` (`offscreen.js:22-29`) abandon fetches that keep holding server slots (`tts.js:53`).
- Estimated gain: a seek or voice change during prefetch currently waits up to 0.55–0.92 s behind a fresh Edge request, or 3.6–7.4 s behind a Supertonic chunk (both durations measured).
- Fix:
  - One AbortController per queue entry.
  - Abort out-of-window entries in `ensureWindow`, and everything on dispose/stop.
  - Swallow AbortError so it doesn't show an error toast.
  - In `tts.js`, skip synthesis if `res.on('close')` fired with `!res.writableEnded` before a slot freed up.
- To measure: a puppeteer script that clicks a sentence in chunk k, then chunk k+10 300 ms later, timing the second click to the first highlighted word (`.w.is-active`).
- Risk: medium, because web and extension must stay in step.

5. **Move Supertonic into a worker thread** (engine, M)
- (e): MP3 encoding with lamejs (`mp3.js:13-31`) is also synchronous: 267 ms per 18 s chunk (`micro.mjs`), 416–939 ms under load.
- Measured (`st-worker-main.mjs`): during one synthesis, the engine's event loop was blocked for up to 490–810 ms (median 397–521 ms) in-process. In a worker it was 17–21 ms (median 5.7 ms), with the same synthesis time (4.0–5.7 s in-process vs 4.5–5.2 s in the worker).
- So today, while Supertonic prefetches, requests from every surface can wait up to that long: cached-chunk seeks, `/api/docs`, `/api/segment`.
- Don't lower the bitrate to save time: at 44.1 kHz, 64 kbps encodes slower (421 ms) than 128 kbps.
- Risk: medium. The worker and the native addon must load from the packaged app bundle; `e2e:desktop` has to cover that.

6. **Reuse Edge WebSockets** (engine, M)
- (c): `edge-tts-universal/dist/index.js:386-403` opens a new socket for every request and closes it when the request finishes (line 423).
- Measured (`edge-bench.mjs`): opening costs 218–428 ms of a 549–921 ms request.
- One socket handled 7 requests in a row (one after 20 s idle) at 270–542 ms each. That saves 230–300 ms (35–45%) per uncached chunk on the default provider.
- Risk: medium. The protocol is unofficial and I only tested idle up to 20 s, so it needs reconnect-and-retry. It must keep the voice allowlist check (`isKnownVoice`), text escaping, the 4096-byte split and the tick-to-ms conversion.

7. **Make `updateTimes` linear** (web, S)
- `chunkMs()` recomputes `msPerChar()` over every recorded duration, once per chunk, every 250 ms (`player.js:290-317`, `372-374`).
- Measured (`updatetimes.mjs`): 11.4 ms per tick at 4,700 chunks and 500 durations. At 9,541 chunks and 2,000 durations it is 80.9 ms per tick, 32% of the main thread, which causes highlight jank and late chunk handoffs.
- Fix: compute `msPerChar()` once per call. Risk: none.

8. **Stream Edge audio for the chunk the user is waiting on** (engine, web, extension; L)
- (b): the server waits for full synthesis. `edge.js:35-47` buffers the whole stream, and `tts.js:59-65` replies with base64 JSON plus words only after writing the cache.
- Measured: on a warm service, first audio arrives at 98–235 ms and completion at 270–542 ms.
- 4 of 35 Edge requests took 5.4–14.5 s. In the two where I logged first audio, it arrived at 3.0–4.1 s, so streaming would have saved 5–10 s there.
- Needs MediaSource playback in both clients (the CSP's `media-src blob:` already allows it); word timings would arrive after the audio starts.

**Smaller items, or no action needed (measured)**
- (g) The base64 decode `Uint8Array.from(atob(b64), fn)` (`queue.js:40`, `offscreen.js:80`) takes 5.9 ms for an Edge chunk and 13.4 ms for a Supertonic chunk; a plain loop takes 0.7 ms. S.
- Edge voice list: 448 ms cold, then 106–140 ms. It is paid before `route()` (`main.js:489`), and by the first voice check after engine start or 24 h cache expiry. Prefetching it at engine start fixes that. S.
- The extension's `onSessionStart` runs segmentation, the voice fetch and offscreen-document creation one after another (`sw.js:201-252`). Running them in parallel should save 50–150 ms, but that is an unmeasured estimate.
- Speed changes cost 0 ms: they only change `playbackRate`, with no re-synthesis.
- The chunk handoff itself is fine: in headless Chrome the next chunk starts 24–26 ms after the previous one ends (20/20 samples). Audible gaps come from embedded silence (item 2) or prefetch falling behind (items 1 and 3).
- (f)/(i) Nothing to gain: hashing takes 1.1 µs, a cache hit 0.18 ms, a cache write ~1 ms, and re-slicing chunk text 0.8 ms even at the 2M-character document cap.

  - The CoreML MLProgram format fails at run time after a 9.8 s compile.
  - The CoreML NeuralNetwork format runs in 2.7–3.7 s versus 3.4–3.8 s on CPU, with 5.9 s extra startup, so no clear gain.
  - Setting the intra-op thread count to 6 or 8 gave no gain.
- (d) Warm-up: loading the Supertonic models the first time takes 469–700 ms, once per engine start.
